**Step 2: You can wash up to 95 degrees.**
Organic cotton is resilient. A hot wash is actually great for hygiene, and natissy liners handle it beautifully. Wash at whatever temperature feels right to you. The cotton holds up.

**Step 3: Skip the fabric softener.**
I know, counterintuitive. But fabric softener slowly coats and breaks down cotton fibers, and reduces absorbency over time. A gentle, fragrance-free detergent is all you need. The cotton does the rest.

**Step 4: Give them some air.**
A breathable cotton pouch or a drawer works perfectly between uses. Just avoid sealed bags. Organic cotton likes to breathe, and a little airflow keeps everything fresh.

**Step 5: Cold water first for stains.**
Always cold, never hot. Hot water sets stains into the fabric permanently. A quick cold soak first, then your normal wash, and you are good.

These five things are the difference between liners that last two years and ones that last eight.

Hey [First Name],

A few things I wish someone had told me about switching to reusable liners:

**Give it two or three washes first.**
New organic cotton gets softer with every wash. If your first wear feels slightly different from what you expected, wash them once before judging. They settle into their best softness within a few uses.

**Pair with breathable underwear.**
Synthetic fabrics underneath can trap heat and reduce the airflow that makes organic cotton so comfortable. Cotton or bamboo underwear works best with natissy.

**Change at the same times you would a disposable.**
Reusable does not mean once a day. Follow the same routine you have always had: change when you feel you need to. Organic cotton manages freshness well but it still needs regular changes.

**The difference shows up on the days you used to notice discomfort most.**
Many women notice it most on the days they were most bothered by conventional liners. Give it a few weeks and pay attention to those days specifically.

## Email 5: Education
**Send:** Day 14
**Subject:** What is actually in a conventional liner
**Subject variant 2:** The ingredients nobody talks about
**Subject variant 3:** Why the irritation happens
**Preview text:** Most women never connect the two. Once you know, you cannot unknow it.

---

Hey [First Name],

Most women spend years dealing with irritation, itching, or discomfort without ever connecting it to what they are wearing every day.

Conventional panty liners contain some or all of the following:

**Chlorine-bleached pulp.**
Used to make the product look white and clean. The process can leave trace residues on the material itself.

**Synthetic fragrances.**
Added to mask odors. Fragrance formulations are considered proprietary, which means brands do not have to disclose what is actually in them. Many contain compounds known to irritate sensitive skin.

**Plastic backing and SAP gel.**
The waterproof layer and the absorbent core in most disposables are plastic or petroleum-based materials. They trap heat, reduce airflow, and sit against the skin for hours.

**Rayon and viscose.**
Synthetic fibers often blended into the soft top layer. They can absorb moisture unevenly and, depending on how they are processed, may carry residue.

This is not meant to alarm you. It is just information most brands do not put on the front of their packaging.

Organic cotton has one ingredient: cotton. Grown without synthetic pesticides and processed without chlorine or added chemicals. That is the whole story.

You already made the switch. I just wanted you to know why it matters.
